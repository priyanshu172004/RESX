# Aurora Components Pvt Ltd — FY2025 Management Review

## 1. Overview

Aurora Components manufactures precision industrial fasteners and sub-assemblies
for the automotive and renewable-energy sectors, operating three plants in
Pune, Coimbatore and Sanand. FY2025 closed with consolidated revenue of
INR 815.0 crore against INR 698.2 crore in FY2024, a growth of 16.7%.

The year was one of expansion against deteriorating unit economics. Volume grew
faster than revenue and revenue grew faster than gross profit, and that ordering
is the central fact of the year.

## 2. Revenue and volume

Revenue rose in all four quarters, but the rate of growth did not hold. Q2 grew
7.9% over Q1 and Q3 grew 9.0% over Q2, while Q4 grew only 3.2% over Q3.
Management attributes the Q4 slowdown to the deferral of two large renewable
orders into FY2026 following a change in state procurement rules.

Units shipped rose 27.7% across the year while revenue rose 21.3% between Q1 and
Q4. Realisation per unit therefore fell from approximately INR 44,272 to
INR 42,072, a decline of 5.0%.

## 3. Margin

Gross margin compressed in every quarter, from 34.2% in Q1 to 29.8% in Q4.
Two causes are identified:

- Alloy steel input prices rose 14% over the year and Aurora's supply contracts
  reprice quarterly, so cost increases reached the P&L with roughly one quarter
  of lag and were not passed to customers.
- The mix shifted toward the renewable-energy segment, which carries structurally
  lower margin than automotive but was pursued for volume.

No price increase was implemented during FY2025. A 4% list price revision was
approved in March 2025 and takes effect in Q1 FY2026.

## 4. Regional performance

North region contributed INR 291.6 crore at 33.8% margin and remains the
strongest business. West region is the concern: INR 172.3 crore at 26.4% margin,
the lowest of the four, and the only region where customer count fell year on
year. The West plant at Sanand ran at 61% utilisation against a company average
of 78%.

## 5. Customer economics

Quarterly churn rose from 4.1% to 7.3%. NPS fell from 47 to 38. Customer
acquisition cost rose 57% from INR 18,400 to INR 28,900 while lifetime value
fell 10.7% from INR 214,000 to INR 191,000. The LTV/CAC ratio consequently
fell from 11.6x to 6.6x.

Exit interviews with departing accounts cite delivery reliability rather than
price. On-time-in-full delivery averaged 87.2% against a contractual commitment
of 95%.

## 6. Cost base

Headcount grew from 412 to 531, an increase of 28.9%, against revenue growth of
21.3% over the same period. Revenue per employee fell from INR 0.443 crore to
INR 0.417 crore. Logistics cost grew 62.2% — materially faster than revenue —
driven by expedited freight used to protect delivery commitments.

R&D spend grew 15.8%, slower than revenue, and now stands at 1.6% of revenue
against a stated policy target of 3.0%.

## 7. Known risks

- Input price exposure is unhedged and contracts reprice quarterly.
- Customer concentration: the top five accounts represent 41% of revenue.
- The Sanand plant's underutilisation carries fixed cost that scales with
  neither volume nor revenue.
- Two renewable orders deferred to FY2026 are not yet contractually confirmed.

## 8. Open questions for the board

- Should the FY2026 price revision be 4%, or does the margin trajectory require
  more?
- Is the West region a turnaround or an exit?
- Should expedited freight continue at current levels given the OTIF gap it is
  covering rather than closing?
